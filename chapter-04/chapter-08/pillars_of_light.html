8.3 · The Pillars of Light

The QFT spectrum, read out as a probability distribution. Measuring the register almost always lands on a pillar at k ≈ j·M/r. From that single integer, continued fractions recover the hidden period r — and from r, the factors of N.

Run a measurement that yields an even r whose halves don't hit ±1.

The trick. A noisy measurement gives only a single k, but k/M is a near-perfect rational approximation to j/r. The continued-fraction expansion of k/M has a convergent whose denominator (less than N) is r. Even peaks need a couple of shots — if k lands at a multiple sharing a factor with j, you get a divisor of r and simply repeat. Defaults a=7, N=15 give r=4 → factors 3 and 5.

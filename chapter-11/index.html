PLANCK · CHAPTER 11

Noise, Decoherence & Error Correction

Everything in Chapters 1–10 assumed a perfect qubit: isolated, immortal, and honest. Real qubits leak, forget, and lie. This chapter is the reality check — and the counter-attack.

How to read this chapter. Work top to bottom: it builds from the one tool the whole chapter needs — the density matrix (11.1) — through how a single qubit decays (11.2), how entanglement dies (11.3), how we fight back with redundancy (11.6) and the surface code (11.7), ending in the full sandbox that ties it all together (11.8). Each page opens on its own.

T₁ and T₂

The two real decay channels on one qubit: T₁ relaxation (energy loss, the vector climbs to the pole) and T₂ dephasing (phase loss, the vector collapses onto the axis). Watch the Bloch vector spiral inward.

Decoherence of entanglement

The same two channels on a Bell pair. The off-diagonal coherences fade, population drains into |00⟩, and entanglement itself — the concurrence — dies. Entanglement is fragile.

Fighting back: the repetition code

Spread one logical bit across three physical qubits; a single flip is caught and majority-voted away. Below a threshold error rate redundancy wins — above it, it hurts. PL = 3p² − 2p³.

Twenty-three terms, in the order the book introduces them: from the captive cloud to the fortress that keeps it alive.

Qubit

The basic unit of quantum information: any two-level quantum system engineered so that its superposition can be deliberately steered and finally read.

The condition of being in a weighted blend of possibilities at once; not ignorance of an answer, but the absence of one.

The complete mathematical description of a quantum state; its entries record the amplitude of every possibility.

The law that links the ledger to the world: the probability of an outcome equals the square of its amplitude’s size.

Each possibility carries a size and a timing. The size sets how likely the footfall is to land there; the timing decides how possibilities reinforce or cancel.

Interference

The reinforcement or cancellation of possibilities when their phases align or oppose; the engine every quantum algorithm runs on.

Decoherence

The loss of superposition through unintended entanglement with the environment; the enemy every quantum machine is built to hold back.

The preserved phase relationships that allow possibilities to interfere; the resource every quantum computation spends, and the one decoherence steals.

Entanglement

A shared state between two or more quantum systems whose properties are correlated more tightly than any classical pair could manage.

A single protected qubit encoded across many physical qubits so that errors can be caught and corrected without looking at the cloud itself.

Interference — the engine of the search

A haystack of answers, all equally likely. Two moves — flip the needle's phase, then mirror every bar across the mean — and watch the wrong answers cancel while the right one piles up. Do it the right number of times and the needle is almost all that's left.

Three operations, one engine. Grover uses superposition (every item equally likely), an oracle (a phase flip that marks the target), and a diffuser (a reflection about the mean that amplifies the marked state). These work through constructive and destructive interference — entanglement is not required for the algorithm to work.

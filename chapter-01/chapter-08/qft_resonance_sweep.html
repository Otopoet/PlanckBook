8.2 · The QFT Resonance Sweep

After modular exponentiation and a measurement of the second register, the input register holds a periodic comb — equal-amplitude spikes spaced r apart. The Quantum Fourier Transform turns that comb in the time domain into sharp resonances in the frequency domain, located at integer multiples of M/r.

FM|x⟩ = (1/√M) · Σk=0…M−1 e2πi·xk/M |k⟩
r = 4 · peaks every M/r = 16

Before QFT — time domain (the periodic comb)

Amplitudes |⟨x|ψ⟩|. Equal-height teeth at x = x₀, x₀+r, x₀+2r, …

▼ apply FM (Quantum Fourier Transform) ▼

After QFT — frequency domain (the resonance spectrum)

Amplitudes |⟨k|FMψ⟩|. Energy collapses onto frequencies k ≈ j·M/r (dashed lines).

Why it works. Each spike of the comb radiates the same phase pattern; the QFT makes those patterns interfere — constructively only where the frequency matches the comb's spacing, k = jM/r. When r divides M the peaks are razor-sharp (try M=64, r=4). When it doesn't, the peaks broaden into clusters around jM/r (try a=2, N=21 with M=64, r=6) — still readable, just leakier. The offset x₀ only rotates phases, so the magnitudes — and the peak positions — never move. Measure this spectrum and you get 8.3.

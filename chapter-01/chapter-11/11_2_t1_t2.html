T₁ and T₂

A real qubit forgets in two ways at once. T₁ is energy loss — the excited state |1⟩ leaks down to the ground state |0⟩, and the Bloch vector climbs toward the north pole. T₂ is phase loss — the transverse x,y components shrink as the superposition's phase randomizes, collapsing the vector onto the z-axis. Press play and watch the state spiral inward and up as its quantumness drains away.

T₁ — energy loss

Amplitude damping pumps the excited state |1⟩ down to the ground state |0⟩. The Bloch vector climbs toward the north pole and the excited population P(1)→0 with time constant T₁.

T₂ — phase loss

Dephasing randomizes the relative phase, so the x,y components shrink. The vector collapses onto the z-axis and coherence C→0 with time constant T₂. T₂ is usually the faster, limiting timescale.

Decoherence destroys the off-axis "quantumness" first. The transverse coherence (T₂) typically dies before the energy fully relaxes (T₁) — which is why the vector flattens onto the axis before it finishes climbing to the pole.
